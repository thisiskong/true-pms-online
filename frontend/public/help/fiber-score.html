How the fiber score works

Every ONU gets a fiber score from 1 (poor) to 10 (excellent) from a daily batch over a 7-day window. It is built from four sub-scores, each between 0 and 1, combined with weights and then limited by caps. An ONU is bad at score 4 or below.

21–3 Poor 44–5 Weak 66–7 Fair 98–10 Good

1. Power 40%

How strong is the received light?

FeatureMeaning
rx_medMedian daily Rx (dBm) over the window
rx_p1010th-percentile Rx, which catches bad days (needs at least 3 days)

Each is mapped to 0–1 and the two are averaged:

Rx (dBm)−30−26−22 to −9−6
Sub-score00.51 (healthy)0 (overload)

These limits do not depend on fiber distance, on purpose: the ONU receiver needs at least −28 dBm however long the link is. So long links naturally score lower on power even when nothing is broken. The excess-loss part (below) is what separates “far away” from “faulty”.

Where expected_rx comes from

A LightGBM regression model trained on the inventory. These features feed the score only through this model. Share of importance from the 2026-11-23 model:

FeatureMeaningImportance
moduleclassOptic class25%
modelONU / OLT model24%
len_kmFiber path length23%
split_dbSplitter loss from the split ratio15%
l1ratioL1 split ratio6%
l2ratioL2 split ratio4%

The model's typical error is about ±1.9 dB, so an excess loss inside that band is noise rather than a fault.

How fiber distance is taken into account

A long link is expected to be weaker, so only the loss beyond that counts against the ONU. Measured on 2.7M clean ONUs (2026-11-23 run), Rx drops by about 0.3–0.4 dB per km, in line with the textbook 0.25–0.35 dB/km for 1490 nm downstream plus splices:

SplitOpticONUsRx loss per kmRx at 0 km
1:64B+452K0.33 dB/km−20.6 dBm
1:32B+349K0.34 dB/km−18.8 dBm
1:28B+202K0.42 dB/km−18.0 dBm
1:64C+159K0.52 dB/km−17.4 dBm
  • The splitter, not the distance, uses most of the budget. A 1:64 split costs about 18–21 dB; 20 km of fiber adds only about 6–7 dB. Length explains only about 5% of the spread in Rx: connectors, splices and optic class vary much more.
  • The length comes from inventory (len_km = (l1len + l2len) / 1000), not from the OLT's measured ranging. For about 6.5% of ONUs the two differ by more than 2 km (range_mismatch), and their expected Rx is less reliable. The inventory length may not include the drop cable.
  • ONUs with no ODN inventory row have no length, so the excess sub-score is missing and its weight is shared out among the others.

3. Errors 20%

Are there line errors on the fiber link? The worse of the two signals counts.

FeatureMeaningMapping to sub-score
bip_log_meanMean of log10(1 + BIP-in errors per day)0 → 1 · 3 → 0.5 · ≥ 5 → 0
bip_days / bip_obsShare of observed days with any error (needs at least 3 days)0 → 1 · 15% → 0.5 · 100% → 0

GCOM and Raisecom ONUs report no BIP counters, so this sub-score is missing for them and its weight is shared out among the other three.

4. Stability 15%

Is the signal steady, or getting worse? The worst of the three signals counts.

FeatureMeaningMapping to sub-score
rx_sdDay-to-day Rx standard deviation≤ 0.5 dB → 1 · 1.5 → 0.5 · ≥ 3 → 0
rx_slopeRx trend (dB per day)≥ −0.05 → 1 · ≤ −0.3 → 0 (fading)
lc_meanRADIUS sessions ended with cause Lost-Carrier, per day≤ 1 → 1 · 5 → 0.3 · ≥ 20 → 0

Lost-carrier is the only RADIUS input to the score. It is a weak fiber signal: 35% of ONUs have at least one event in a week, mostly the customer switching the ONU off. That is why it only counts at 5 or more events a day and is one of three stability inputs.

Putting it together

score = round(1 + 9 × weighted average of the sub-scores that exist)

Caps (applied after the weighted score)

ConditionMaximum score
rx_med below −28 dBm (receiver sensitivity)3
rx_med below −30 dBm1
BIP errors on every observed day (at least 3 days observed)5

A cap is why an ONU can show a low score even when its other sub-scores look fine. The ONU drawer lists the cap that applied.

Confidence

conf = (share of the total weight that had data) × (share of the window's days observed)

Confidence is not part of the score. A score with confidence below 0.5 is shown as a hollow chip: treat it with care.

Not part of the score

These are computed for every ONU but only used to decide where a fault is and as notes in the explanation:

  • peer_delta_l2, peer_delta_l1: the ONU's Rx compared with its siblings on the same L2 / L1
  • hw_flag: ONU Tx, voltage, temperature or current out of range (likely the ONU, not the fiber)
  • range_mismatch, dq_no_odn, dq_topo_mismatch, dq_l2_multi_device: inventory and data-quality flags

Suspect segments and “likely location”

OLT ── PON port ── L1 splitter (feeder) ── L2 splitter ── drop cable ── ONU “L1” in the UI “L2” in the UI

A suspect segment is a shared part of the fiber tree where bad ONUs cluster more than chance explains, which points to one fault there rather than separate problems at each home.

Suspect L2 segment: all three must hold

  • At least 2 bad ONUs
  • p-value ≤ 0.01 against the network-wide bad rate (about 1.2%)
  • Either at least half its ONUs are bad, or its median Rx is at least 3 dB worse than the other L2s on the same L1

L2 names that appear on more than one OLT are skipped as an inventory error.

Likely location of each bad ONU

Each bad ONU is assigned to the highest level that explains it, so it is counted once:

Likely locationPattern
OLTMost PONs on the OLT are bad: optics batch, configuration, or site power/temperature
L1 / PONOne PON is bad, its sibling PONs are fine, and the bad ONUs are spread over its L2s. May be the OLT PON optic, since L1 maps 1:1 to the PON port
L2 segmentThe ONU's L2 is a suspect: L2 splitter or distribution fiber
ONU hardwareIsolated bad ONU with hw_flag set
Drop / connectorIsolated bad ONU whose siblings are healthy

Impact (how suspects are ranked)

impact = bad ONUs attributed × (1 + median excess loss of the bad ONUs, dB)

Higher impact means one repair is likely to fix more customers.
